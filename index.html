The scenario

British Grand Prix week

Half a million fans come through the gates. A permanent team of 356 leads casual teams and 594 volunteer Race Makers. Managers promoted in the spring are leading people they met that week, and senior managers are making commercial decisions in real time.

What happens next depends on how those managers were developed, how clearly they think under pressure, and whether the learning behind them joins up. That is what this playbook is for.

Step one · Successful scoping

SEEN: our bespoke scoping, skills scan and TNA

Nothing is designed until we understand what is really happening. Scoping typically takes the first thirty days and runs alongside your L&D Partner from the first conversation.

1

Listen

Conversations with the executive team, the People team, your L&D Partner and the head of each department. What does good management look like here, and where does it break down?

2

Observe

Time on site on an event day and on a quiet day. How managers brief, decide and lead when it is busy tells us more than any survey.

3

Skills scan and TNA

A short skills scan for managers at every level, with personalised diagnostics that show the gaps and the strengths already in the business.

4

Tools review

Every learning platform and tool listed in one place: who uses it, what it costs, where it overlaps and what is missing.

5

Blueprint

Root causes, priorities and measures on a single page, agreed with stakeholders before any design begins.

Why this works

The business invests only where it matters. Managers recognise their own world in the findings, so they commit to the change. And because the measures are agreed before design, everyone knows what success looks like. That is what creates lasting change.

Step two · The priorities

Four priorities, built in the order that makes each one easier

These follow your brief. The detail will be shaped by what scoping finds, so this is the starting blueprint, not the finished design.

Priority one

One management pathway, from first team to commercial leader

Three connected stages with the same language running through them, so a manager can see where they are and what comes next. Each stage is a blend of short face to face sessions, micro learning, guest speakers, practice on real Silverstone situations and coaching back on the job.

Stage one

New Manager Essentials

  • Moving from team member to manager
  • Setting expectations and everyday conversations
  • Leading casual, seasonal and volunteer teams
  • Staying clear headed on event days
  • Employment law essentials
Stage two

Established managers

  • Coaching for Success and a feedback culture
  • Probation and performance conversations
  • Leading across generations
  • Planning and prioritising around the calendar
  • Mentoring new managers
Stage three

Strategic and commercial leaders

  • Commercial awareness across events, experiences and hospitality
  • Decisions made with data
  • Leading change and growth
  • Identity as a leader
  • Influencing at executive level

Employment law changes, built in at every stage

  • Day one rights. Paternity and unpaid parental leave from the first day, and sick pay from the first day of illness. Managers need to handle these conversations well from week one.
  • Preventing harassment. A stronger duty to take all reasonable steps, including harassment by customers and guests. For a venue welcoming two million people a year, this is a daily management skill.
  • Dismissal protection after six months. Expected from January 2027. Probation, early feedback and good records matter far more than before.
  • Zero and low hours contracts. Guaranteed hours offers, reasonable notice of shifts and payment for cancelled shifts are expected during 2027. Directly relevant to anyone leading casual teams.
  • Flexible working and bereavement leave. New processes expected during 2027 that every manager will need to follow with confidence.

We design how managers learn and practise this, through real scenarios. The legal content itself is agreed with your People team and legal advisers, and every date is checked against the government timetable before delivery.

Priority three

A joined up learning ecosystem

The tools review from scoping becomes a clear recommendation: what to keep, what to combine and what to retire. The aim is one front door for learning, so a manager can find what they need in a minute.

  • One map of every platform, tool and programme, with usage and cost.
  • The management pathway, AI learning, compliance, Changing Lanes, Early Careers and Race Maker training connected, not sitting apart.
  • Simple ownership: who updates what, and how new content is added.
  • Data your L&D Partner can pull without a specialist, so progress is visible to the business.
Priority four

Built to last after we leave

We upskill internally, so delivery becomes more profitable and more autonomous for your teams. The measure of success is how little you need us at the end.

  • Train the Trainer. Internal facilitators learn the principles behind every session, not just the slides. It can run alongside a proof of concept.
  • Facilitator playbooks. Every module handed over with session plans, scenarios and guidance, ready to repeat.
  • Coaching for Success. A coaching and feedback culture, so managers keep the new habits alive in everyday conversations.
  • A skills matrix. A simple tool for managers to coach alongside, showing progress by person and by team.
  • Learning champions. One in every department, keeping learning alive between sessions and feeding back what works.
How people will learn

Short, varied and built around the working day

Nobody at Silverstone has spare days to sit in a classroom. The learning is designed in small pieces that fit around the calendar, and it upskills your internal team at the same time.

Micro learning

Short, focused pieces of ten to fifteen minutes, on one skill at a time. Easy to fit in before a shift, and easy to return to when it is needed.

Guest speakers

Voices from inside and outside the business who have led through pressure, growth and change. They bring the stretch and make the learning memorable.

Workshops and practice

Interactive sessions on real Silverstone situations, with diagnostics, scenarios and positive challenge in a psychologically safe space.

Internal trainers and champions

Your own people are upskilled to deliver, coach and champion the learning in every department. This is how it becomes yours.

Behaviour and habits

Clear thinking under pressure is a skill, and it can be developed

Event days keep people at the edge of their Window of Tolerance. Outside it, judgement, tone and accuracy drop. Our Self Regulation framework gives managers a shared language and a practical way back, always tied to a behaviour and an outcome.

Above the windowRushing, snapping, missing details when the gates open.
Inside the windowCalm, accurate and clear with teams and guests.
Below the windowSwitching off, going quiet, waiting for someone else to act.

The Navigator

Momentum and quick decisions.

Under pressure

Decides fast and skips the final check.

The outcome we want

Pause, confirm, then act. Fewer errors on the day.

The Anchor

Steady and reliable.

Under pressure

Holds back a problem rather than raising it.

The outcome we want

Speaks up early, so issues are escalated in time.

The Architect

Clarity and structure.

Under pressure

Waits for more information before deciding.

The outcome we want

Decides with enough, not everything.

The Connector

Warmth with people.

Under pressure

Absorbs every guest's frustration.

The outcome we want

Warm with boundaries. Steady service and better wellbeing.

Learning as a collective

Learning is not the sole responsibility of L&D

In Modern Learning, Kay explores why learning needs collective support. It is why we co design with the business, so the changes carry the right internal voice and blueprint.

  • Multiple generations at one table. Experienced managers and first time managers learn together, so knowledge and fresh thinking cross pollinate.
  • Mentors in the room. People who have led through a Grand Prix weekend share how they handled it and coach in the moment.
  • Across departments. Building on Changing Lanes, managers from Events, Escapade, Karting and Commercial solve the same problems together.
  • Shared ownership. Leaders, mentors and champions own the outcomes alongside your L&D Partner. That is how learning sticks.
  • Strong relationships. Working this way builds trust across departments and levels, and those relationships carry the work long after the programme.
